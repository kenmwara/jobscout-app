Five changes, and one of them is the whole idea. The score leads — 52px, first in reading order, where the eye lands; it shipped as a bare numeral parked in a corner at body-text weight, so the most important value on the card was the least weighted. The title moves to Newsreader: the phone shipped it in sans-bold while the desktop card already obeyed law 13. Metadata gets three tiers — band pill filled in the band colour, location chip sunken with no hue, date with no container at all — because a verdict and a fact were rendered identically. The heart leaves the score’s corner for the action row, so saving stops competing with the verdict. And the card carries one evidence line, the strongest: what-to-answer stays behind the tap, because a browse card that leads with a gap is leading with what the reader lacks, which law 12 forbids. The idea: an unscored card is the same card with an unlit rose. Eight empty bearings say “there is a score to be had here” without a word, on every row of the sweep — and when a resume arrives they fill in place. Browse becomes matches.
